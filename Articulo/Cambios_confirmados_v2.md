## Bloque 26 — Refuerzo metodológico en 2.5 (respuesta a duda de Alexis sobre lexicográfico/epsilon-constraint)

**26.1 — Sección 2.5, justificación del paso lexicográfico**
- Buscar: `A lexicographic payoff table first establishes the extremes of each objective; f1 is then maximized subject to a progressively increasing floor on f2, with the floor varied over 10 intervals between those extremes, following the epsilon-constraint implementation of Mavrotas (2009).`
- Reemplazar por: `A lexicographic payoff table first establishes the extremes of each objective, rather than a naive one: multiple solutions can tie on f1 alone, and an arbitrarily selected tie among them need not be Pareto efficient in f2; the lexicographic step resolves this by maximizing f2 as a secondary criterion among f1-optimal solutions, anchoring the front at genuinely efficient extremes rather than weakly efficient ones. f1 is then maximized subject to a progressively increasing floor on f2, with the floor varied over 10 intervals between those extremes, following the epsilon-constraint implementation of Mavrotas (2009).`

**26.2 — Sección 2.5, justificación de epsilon-constraint sobre suma ponderada**
- Buscar: `The epsilon constraint method was preferred over weighted sum aggregation because it controls the number of efficient solutions, does not skip non-convex regions of the front, and avoids arbitrary normalization of objectives expressed in different units.`
- Reemplazar por: `The epsilon constraint method (Haimes et al., 1971) was preferred over weighted sum aggregation for three reasons: it controls the number of efficient solutions directly, rather than through arbitrarily chosen weights, which would require assigning a qualitative preference between economic and environmental-social performance that this study does not presume to make; it does not skip non-convex regions of the front, which weighted sums can miss entirely; and it avoids arbitrary normalization of objectives expressed in different units. The resulting front also serves a further purpose beyond identifying efficient solutions: because each point corresponds to a specific, interpretable deployment level, the front itself visualizes how sensitively profitability responds to marginal increases in deployment, information a single weighted-sum solution would not reveal.`
